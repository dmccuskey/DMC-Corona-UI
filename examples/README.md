# Examples

Each app folder is a complete Solar2D project with its own copy of the library: open its `main.lua` in the Solar2D Simulator. The apps are grouped by the widget or control they show; `*-memtest` apps create and remove widgets in a loop to check memory use, with [dmc-performance](https://github.com/dmccuskey/dmc-performance).

## ScrollView

| | |
|---|---|
| <img src="screenshots/scrollview-simple.png" width="240" alt="scrollview-simple: a scroll view filling the screen below the status bar, showing rows 2 to 8 of its content, numbered colored bands, with a scroll indicator at the right edge"> | **scrollview-simple**: a scroll view which fills the screen below the status bar, sized from what the device reports (`dUI.WIDTH`, `dUI.HEIGHT`, `display.topStatusBarContentHeight`), over 20 numbered rows (`NUM_ROWS`, `ROW_HEIGHT`), masked to its size (`autoMask`). Drag or flick it up and down; it bounces at the edges. The rows fit its width, so `horizontalScrollEnabled=false`: content that fits can otherwise still be pulled sideways. After a second it scrolls to row 2 (`setContentPosition()`, the screenshot); after four scrolling is locked (`verticalScrollEnabled`); after eight it is back, and the content rests 10 higher (`upperVerticalOffset`). The scroll indicator on the right shows while the content moves. |
| <img src="screenshots/scrollview-zoom.png" width="240" alt="scrollview-zoom: a photo of a harbor at sunset, zoomed out so that all of it fits across the screen, black above and below"> | **scrollview-zoom**: a photo of 1024x680 in a scroll view which fills the screen below the status bar; at full size it scrolls in both directions. The delegate's `getViewForZoom()` names the photo as the object to scale; `minimumZoom` is the scale at which all of it fits, `maximumZoom` twice its size. After a second the app zooms out to show the whole photo (`setZoomScale()`, the screenshot), after four back in to full size; then drag to move around, pinch to zoom (on a device: the Simulator has one touch). Its scroll indicators are light (`indicatorColor` in its style), to show on the dark photo. |
| <img src="screenshots/scrollview-memtest.png" width="240" alt="scrollview-memtest: an empty white scroll view on a black screen"> | **scrollview-memtest**: creates an empty scroll view and removes it 200 ms later, over and over, and prints memory use with [dmc-performance](https://github.com/dmccuskey/dmc-performance); `run_example1()` does the same with a scroll view style. |

## TableView

Each table view fills the screen below the status bar, sized from what the device reports (`dUI.WIDTH`, `dUI.HEIGHT`, `display.topStatusBarContentHeight`), and is masked to its size (`autoMask`).

| | |
|---|---|
| <img src="screenshots/tableview-simple.png" width="240" alt="tableview-simple: a white list filling the screen below the status bar, rows 1 to 12 with a thin gray line under each"> | **tableview-simple**: a list of 50 rows (`NUM_ROWS`, `ROW_HEIGHT`). A delegate supplies them: how many there are (`numberOfRows`), and what a row shows when the table view asks for it (`onRowRender`); only the rows near the screen exist. Drag or flick it up and down; it bounces at the edges, and a scroll indicator shows on the right while it moves. Tap a row and the app prints its number (`didSelectRow`); when the list comes to rest it prints where, and whether that is the top or the bottom (`didEndScrolling`). |
| <img src="screenshots/tableview-modify.png" width="240" alt="tableview-modify: ten gray rows with red lines, row 5 reading 'inserted' and row 10 'added at the end', dark blue below them"> | **tableview-modify**: rows added and removed. The list starts with 8 rows, fewer than fill the view; after two seconds a row is inserted at 5 (`insertRowAt()`), after four another is added below the last one (the screenshot), after six row 5 is removed again (`removeRowAt()`). Each row shows its number and the name of its item, so the rows below a change count up or down. The rows are gray with a red line (`setBackgroundColor()`, `setLineColor()` on the row); below them the table view's own color shows (`fillColor` in its style). |
| <img src="screenshots/tableview-scroll.png" width="240" alt="tableview-scroll: rows 179425 to 179436 of a white list, with row 179431 in yellow in the middle of the screen"> | **tableview-scroll**: a list of 250,000 rows. After a second and a half it scrolls for three seconds until row 179,431 is in the middle of the view (`scrollToRowAt()` with `position='middle'` and a `time`); that row is yellow (the screenshot). On the way the rows pass too fast to read: the table view only makes the ones near the screen. |
| <img src="screenshots/tableview-tableviewcell.png" width="240" alt="tableview-tableviewcell: a list of rows, each with a flag, a bold title, a gray detail line and a chevron on the right"> | **tableview-tableviewcell**: `newTableViewCell()` rows, each with a flag (`imageView`), a title and a detail line (the subtitle layout) and a disclosure indicator (the default accessory). One style from `newTableViewCellStyle()` sizes and places the text for rows of 50. The cells are reused: a row which leaves the screen puts its cell in a cache, and the next row to appear takes it. Touch a row and it is highlighted (the style's `active` state); tap it and the app prints its number. |
| <img src="screenshots/tableview-memtest.png" width="240" alt="tableview-memtest: a list of rows with a bold title, a gray detail line and a chevron, in the cell's default small font"> | **tableview-memtest**: creates a table view with TableViewCell rows and removes it 100 ms later, with its cells, over and over, and prints memory use with [dmc-performance](https://github.com/dmccuskey/dmc-performance); `run_example1()` to `3()` do the same with a table view style, a cell style and a single cell. For the screenshot, a table view was kept on the screen. |
